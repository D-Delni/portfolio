# Principle of Questioning Tradition.
You don’t innovate by polishing the past.

![Jobs once called Ive his “spiritual partner at Apple.](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/56cecb5ca3360c3f9d45c441/1456817769297-E3ZGMJZATIASJ79ADVCA/young-steve-jobs-1_0.jpg)

## Most systems are built on dead assumptions. 

This mindset allowed Jobs to rethink everything—from hardware design and operating systems to how packaging, supply chains, and customer service should work. The implication is not just about minimalism or aesthetics; it’s about intellectual integrity. By starting from zero and stripping down assumptions, you expose unnecessary complexity and reveal a clearer, more purposeful design.
He rejected the myth of the solitary genius working in isolation. Instead, he favored teams composed of people with very different backgrounds—engineers, designers, artists, philosophers, musicians—who challenged each other and pushed ideas beyond their comfort zones.

He compared the process to stones rubbing together: the friction isn’t smooth, but it’s what sharpens and polishes the product.

<strong>
“When you get different perspectives to collide—people from different disciplines—you get a lot of arguing, but also sparks of insight. The best ideas come from that dynamic.”
</strong>

At NeXT, Jobs fostered these mixed teams not just as a management choice, but as a creative necessity. He believed this collision of perspectives created new ideas that no single-minded team could ever produce. It was about using tension productively—embracing disagreement not as dysfunction but as a driver of refinement and originality.

<strong>Creation isn’t just about output. It’s a process of becoming. The best work shapes the maker as much as the audience. A founder builds a startup to prove they can. A writer wrestles an idea into clarity. You don’t just create heavy things. You become someone who can. </strong>
